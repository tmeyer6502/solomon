### Major Resume Sections

#### Here are the main sections you would probably want on your resume

Name, Contact Information, LinkedIn, Github/Portfolio

Professional Summary / Objective: 3-4 lines summarizing your years of experience, key tech skills, and achievements.

Technical Skills: Group by category, and list only those relevant to the job; tangental skills are OK

Professional Experience: Reverse chronological order.  Use bullet points and action statements, quantifying where possible ("Saved $30M in e-waste fees")

Education & Certifications: Degrees (GPA if desired), vendor coursework, certifications

Optional: Open source contributions, professional memberships, public mentions etc.

### General Good Advice

You may feel your employment section is weak (especially if you are Junior level or still a student). Instead of
having an "Employment," section (or above that section), have a section called "Projects," or similar. 
That would put the focus on the things
you can do, or have done.

Only list skills you are comfortable answering questions about during an interview. It's better to stick to
your core competencies (and list fewer skills) than risk feeling embarrassed by questions outside your 
comfort zone.

Use minimal formatting--some resumes are very layout- and graphic-intensive, which may cause difficulty for
an ATS (or even an opinionated human). Some people even print them out.

Send a PDF; avoid *.docx files as some systems/people are worried about word macro viruses and won't open the file.

Proofread this thing several times, and have your friends/family do as well.  Refine and condense often.

If there are a couple of different roles you might be interested in, it's a good idea to create a few versions
of your resume, highlighting different aspects of your skillset.

Remember to always tell the truth. Resume fraud and/or padding is real; you don't want to be one of those
people, tempting though it might be!
